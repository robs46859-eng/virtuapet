## Colorado vs. Delaware

### Recommendation

Colorado is reasonable if the first funding will mainly be founder money, angels, SAFEs, or convertible notes. It is inexpensive and simple when the company operates in Colorado.

Delaware is usually the better starting state if VirtuaPet expects an institutional seed or Series A soon. Venture investors and their lawyers commonly use Delaware corporations and standard Delaware preferred-stock documents. A Colorado company may be required to convert to Delaware before a priced round, adding legal work and cost.

Because the present request is to form **VirtuaPet Inc. in Colorado**, this draft provides room for common and preferred stock. Startup counsel should review the share provision before it is filed.

### Costs and tradeoffs

| Item | Colorado corporation | Delaware corporation operating in Colorado |
|---|---:|---:|
| Incorporation | $50 online | At least $109 for a basic one-page stock corporation; share structure and extra pages can increase it |
| Registered agent | $0 if Robert Smith qualifies and serves | A Delaware agent is mandatory and normally charges an annual commercial fee |
| Colorado authority | Included | Separate Colorado foreign-entity registration is normally required |
| Annual report | $25 Colorado Periodic Report | $50 Delaware annual report, plus Colorado reporting after foreign registration |
| Franchise tax | No Colorado share-count franchise tax | Minimum $175 under the Authorized Shares Method or $400 under the Assumed Par Value Capital Method; use the lower applicable calculation |
| Fundraising | Works for founders, angels, SAFEs, notes, and negotiated stock rounds | More familiar for institutional preferred-stock rounds |
| Administration | One state | Two states, two compliance calendars, and higher annual cost |
| Later conversion | Possible before institutional financing | Usually avoids a later Delaware conversion |

These figures exclude lawyers, tax advisers, registered-agent services, licenses, securities filings, and taxes. Verify fees again on the day of filing.

### Colorado advantages

- Low filing and annual-report fees.
- No commercial Delaware registered agent is needed.
- Simpler administration for a Colorado-based company.
- No annual state filing tax based on authorized share count.

### Colorado disadvantages

- An institutional investor may require Delaware conversion.
- Colorado financing documents are less familiar to many venture firms.
- Conversion later can require filings, approvals, document replacement, and legal fees.

### Delaware advantages

- Familiar to venture funds and startup lawyers.
- Established corporate law and a specialized business court.
- Commonly used standard preferred-stock, option-plan, merger, and acquisition documents.

### Delaware disadvantages

- Higher formation and annual costs.
- Mandatory Delaware registered agent.
- VirtuaPet would likely still register and report in Colorado.
- Large authorized-share counts can cause a high franchise-tax notice unless the proper calculation method is used.

## Recommended Colorado share structure

Select **More than one or other classes of shares**.

Authorize:

| Class | Authorized shares | Par value |
|---|---:|---|
| Common Stock | 10,000,000 | No par value |
| Preferred Stock | 5,000,000 | No par value |
| Total | 15,000,000 | - |

Ten million common shares provide useful whole numbers for founders, employees, advisers, and an option pool. Five million preferred shares reserve room for priced financing. Authorized shares are only the maximum that may be issued; they are not automatically owned or outstanding.

Do not issue every common share to the founder. Founder issuance, vesting, price, option pool, intellectual-property assignment, and tax elections require separate documents and approvals.

SAFEs and convertible notes are not shares when signed. They may convert during a later financing. Every offer or sale of shares, SAFEs, notes, or options must comply with securities laws.

Colorado law allows articles to authorize the board to establish preferred series, but an amendment stating the final series terms must be filed before those shares are issued. A priced-round investor will normally negotiate liquidation preference, conversion, voting, anti-dilution, protective provisions, dividends, redemption, information, and participation rights.

## Proposed share attachment

The aggregate number of shares that the Corporation is authorized to issue is fifteen million (15,000,000), consisting of ten million (10,000,000) shares designated Common Stock and five million (5,000,000) shares designated Preferred Stock.

Each outstanding share of Common Stock shall be entitled to one vote on each matter submitted to shareholders, except as otherwise required by law or provided in a duly filed amendment establishing a series of Preferred Stock.

Subject to the Colorado Business Corporation Act, the Board of Directors is authorized, before issuance, to determine in whole or in part the designation, preferences, limitations, and relative rights of one or more series of Preferred Stock, including dividend rights, conversion rights, voting rights, redemption terms, liquidation preferences, and the number of shares in each series. Before issuing shares whose terms are determined under this authority, the Corporation shall file the articles of amendment required by Colorado law.

No shareholder shall have a preemptive right to acquire unissued shares or other securities except as provided in a written agreement approved by the Corporation.

## Required work after formation

1. Save the filed articles, receipt, Colorado entity ID, and certificate of good standing.
2. Obtain an EIN directly from the IRS.
3. Adopt bylaws and initial incorporator and board actions.
4. Approve founder stock, vesting, purchase price, and intellectual-property assignment.
5. Maintain a capitalization table and stock ledger.
6. Ask a tax professional about a Section 83(b) election before issuing vesting founder shares; its deadline is generally 30 days after transfer.
7. Open a corporate bank account and keep company money separate.
8. Complete applicable tax, wage, sales-tax, licensing, and local registrations.
9. Calendar the Colorado Periodic Report.
10. Before fundraising, approve financing documents and securities-law exemptions.
